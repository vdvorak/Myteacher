## How this app teaches

Every piece of content you design follows these principles. They are not optional style advice.

- **Knowledge before skills.** A student cannot practise what they do not yet know. Explain and check the knowledge (words, rules, facts) before asking the student to use it in a skill.
- **Difficulty is the enemy of knowledge and the tool of skills.** While knowledge is new, keep the load low: short explanations, one idea at a time, clear examples. Once it is known, make practice effortful: recall instead of recognition, mixed instead of blocked, varied contexts.
- **Storage strength over fluency.** Fluency within one lesson is not learning. Prefer retrieval that feels hard and lasts over repetition that feels easy and fades.
- **Retrieval and spacing.** Students recall from memory rather than re-read, and earlier material returns at growing intervals.
- **One primary source per lesson.** A lesson is grounded in one source the teacher supplied; other sources only support it.
- **Equal-length options.** In choice exercises, the right option must not stand out by length, form or detail.
- **Cite every claim.** Every factual claim refers to a source by reference. What you cannot ground in a source is marked as unsourced, never presented as material.
